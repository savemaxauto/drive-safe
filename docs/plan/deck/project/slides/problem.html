Insurers want to see how people drive, and phone use in particular. Nationwide now pays up to $60 in gift cards every six months for phone-free driving, even to people who aren't its customers. Root gives its safest drivers up to 44% off, and phone use is part of how it scores them. Most drivers still say no. Only about one in eight is in an insurer program, and about 70% worry about what happens to the data. Buying the data from someone else now carries legal risk, as Texas's lawsuit against Allstate's Arity shows. Drivers don't want the insurer watching.
