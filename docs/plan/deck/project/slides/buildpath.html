Stage costs from design firm and lab price lists (search summaries). Full plan: docs/product/development-plan.md.

This is the standard way hardware gets built, and each step has a gate so we never spend the big money before the small test says go. The most important step is the second one. For three to eight thousand dollars and about two weeks, we build rough versions of each mechanism and test them in real cars: can you smell the burst from the driver’s seat, does pressing it stress the vent, does anything drip onto the dash, does it survive 85 degrees, and does every phone read the tag. That test decides which build we make. The big spend is the molds, about $14 to $80 thousand, and that only happens after the design is frozen. The basic build reaches production in four to eight months, press-to-puff in six to eleven, and the powered version in seven to twelve. One more thing for counsel: two patents cover an NFC tag on a phone holder that turns on a driving mode, so they need a proper review before we launch any version.
